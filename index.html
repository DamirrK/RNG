Find your seed

Your trainer blinks on a fixed schedule that comes straight from the game’s RNG. Record 40 blinks and this works out the exact RNG state.

How to do this
  1. Load your save and stand somewhere quiet where nothing else moves: no NPCs, no Pokémon following you, no other people walking about. Inside your house works well.
  2. Don’t touch the controller once you start. Opening menus or walking changes the timing.
  3. Turn off the Switch’s auto-sleep and set your phone’s screen to stay on.
  4. Watch your trainer’s eyes. Most blinks are single. Every so often you’ll see a quick double blink.
  5. Record 40 blinks with any method below: tap along, point the camera at the screen live, or film a clip first and upload it. It takes about five to ten minutes, as blinks are a few seconds apart on average.
  6. If it can’t find a seed, a blink was missed or doubled up. Start again; it happens to everyone the first few times.

Already have a seed?

Paste it from another tool as four 32-bit hex values, or two 64-bit values. You’ll need to line up (step 3) before it can track.

Pick your target

Search the advances ahead of you for the Pokémon you want, then tap a result to lock it in.

How to choose
  • The RNG moves about one advance per second while you stand still, so a target 3,600 advances away is roughly an hour of waiting.
  • Shinies are 1 in 4,096 per advance. Searching 10,000 ahead usually turns up two or so. Adding IV or nature filters makes them rarer.
  • Pick something with at least five minutes of lead time so you can walk to the Pokémon and line up.
  • Don’t demand perfect IVs on a shiny. All six at 31 plus shiny is roughly 1 in 134 million for a legendary. Use “Rank results by IVs” instead and take the top result you can wait for.
  • Synchronize only fixes the nature if your lead has it and its nature matches what you pick here.

Line up

Walking and menus advance the RNG by amounts nobody can predict, so once you’re standing in front of the Pokémon, record a few blinks to find exactly where you are, then keep recording to stay synced.

How to do this
  1. Walk up to the Pokémon so the next A press starts the encounter. Don’t save in between.
  2. Stop touching the controller.
  3. If the Pokémon’s face is on screen and it blinks, tick the box below. Its blinks also move the RNG and the maths needs to know.
  4. Record 10 of your trainer’s blinks. Fewer may match in more than one place.
  5. Once it finds you, keep going. Every new blink re-syncs the countdown, which matters most when the Pokémon is blinking: its blinks make long-range prediction unreliable, but watching right up to the press makes it accurate.
  6. The camera can watch the whole wait for you. With tapping, you only need to tap through the last few minutes: tap “Start over”, line up again, and keep tapping on step 4.
  7. If you stop recording, don’t pick up where you left off, because the missing blinks will throw it out. Tap “Start over” instead.

Too far away? Flip the Pokédex

Flipping between Pokédex entries burns through advances far faster than standing still. Nobody can count flips exactly without modding the Switch, so you flip in bursts and line up after each one to see where you landed.

How to flip
  1. Stand in front of the Pokémon first, so you don’t need to walk anywhere afterwards.
  2. Line up as usual so the app knows where you are. The box below tells you how far you have to go.
  3. Tap “Start flipping”, then open the menu, go into the Pokédex and scroll back and forth between entries. A well-filled Pokédex works best.
  4. When the time it suggests is up, close the Pokédex and tap “Stop”. The first burst should be about 20 seconds so it can learn your speed.
  5. Record blinks again above. It finds where you landed, works out how fast you flip, and suggests the next burst.
  6. Stop flipping when you’re within about 600 advances (around 10 minutes), then just wait while recording blinks.
  7. If the Pokédex gets laggy after a few minutes of scrolling, close it and open it again.
  8. Overshoot the target and it’s gone: go back to step 2 and pick a later one. That’s why the app always aims you short.

How to press
  1. Hold the controller with your thumb resting on A.
  2. Press A when the countdown hits zero. The highlighted bar shows your window; anywhere inside it lands the same advance, so you have close to a full second.
  3. Save before the battle only if you’re happy to lose the timing. Most people just reset if it misses.
  4. After the encounter, check its nature and IVs (an IV judge or calculator helps) and tap the matching row below. The app learns your offset for next time.

Fine-tuning

The game offset is how many advances pass between pressing A and the Pokémon being made. It differs between encounters, so start at 0 and let the “What did you get?” table set it. Reaction time is applied to taps and to your A press.
